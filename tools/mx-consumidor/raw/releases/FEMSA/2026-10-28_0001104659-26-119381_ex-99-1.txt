Monterrey, Mexico, October 28, 2026
— Fomento Económico Mexicano, S.A.B. de C.V. (“FEMSA”) (NYSE: FMX; BMV: FEMSAUBD, FEMSAUB) announced today its
operational and financial results for the third quarter of 2026.
Reporting Segments Update: In our continuous
effort to improve our disclosure, we have updated FEMSA’s reporting segment structure to better reflect the scale, stage of development,
and strategic differentiation of our various operations. This updated structure should provide investors with greater visibility
into the drivers of performance across our operations. Our updated reporting segments are as follows: i) OXXO Mexico; ii) Americas &
Mobility which now includes all OXXO convenience store operations outside of Mexico (Brazil, Colombia, Chile, Peru and the U.S.),
as well as the fuel operations in Mexico and the U.S; iii) Europe; iv) Health; and v) Coca-Cola FEMSA. Only segments i) and
ii) changed relative to our previous reporting structure.

Jose Antonio Fernández Garza-Lagüera,
FEMSA’s Chief Executive Officer, commented:
“During the third quarter we delivered a
strong set of results, led by OXXO Mexico and Coca-Cola FEMSA, our two largest businesses. OXXO Mexico continued to build on the positive
momentum seen in recent quarters, while Coca-Cola FEMSA delivered strong growth across the income statement, mainly supported by solid
performances in most of its territories.
We are particularly encouraged by the continued
recovery at OXXO Mexico, where same-store sales accelerated and customer traffic delivered its strongest performance in more than two
years. This result was driven largely by stronger execution in most regions, commercial initiatives focused on key traffic-driving categories,
and the consumer-centric strategy we began implementing last year. These initiatives are gaining traction and allowing us to generate
additional traffic while maintaining a disciplined approach to profitability. Beyond OXXO Mexico, Coca-Cola FEMSA delivered a very strong
quarter, with solid volume trends and double-digit growth in revenues and operating income, reflecting broad-based contributions across
its operations.
We also continued to see encouraging progress
in some of our growth platforms, with Bara gaining momentum as it accelerates expansion and strengthens the performance of its growing
store base, while our OXXO operations in South America maintained a solid commercial trajectory, especially in Colombia. However, certain
other business units faced operating headwinds, particularly our Europe and Health divisions, which delivered mixed results. We continue
to work hard to bring those business units to their potential.
As we enter the final quarter of the year, we
remain mindful that the consumer environment in Mexico continues to be soft, Brazil has faced some volatility recently and that we will
face a more demanding comparison base in the coming quarters. Nevertheless, we are encouraged by the momentum we are seeing across most
of our businesses. While we recognize that the final stretch of the year will present its share of challenges, we believe the strength
of our geographically diversified platform positions us well to continue executing against our long-term strategy in pursuit of sustainable,
profitable growth.”

Total revenues increased 9.8% in 3Q26 compared
to 3Q25, driven by growth in Coca-Cola FEMSA, OXXO Mexico, Americas & Mobility and Health, partially offset by a decrease in
Europe. Excluding the consolidation of OXXO Brazil and currency effects, revenues grew 9.4% on a comparable basis and all the reported
segments grew.
Gross profit increased 9.7%, while gross
margin remained stable at 39.9%. This reflected margin expansion in Coca-Cola FEMSA and Americas & Mobility, offset by contractions
in OXXO Mexico, Europe and Health. The decline in margin in OXXO Mexico resulted in part from a demanding comparison base as the third
quarter of 2025 included a particularly high concentration of commercial income. On a comparable basis, gross profit increased 9.8%.
Income from operations increased 9.9%,
driven by growth in Coca-Cola FEMSA and OXXO Mexico, partially offset by declines in Americas & Mobility, Europe and Health.
The consolidated operating margin reached 8.5%, representing a 10 basis-point expansion year-over-year, reflecting margin expansion at
Coca-Cola FEMSA, partially offset by margin contractions at OXXO Mexico, Americas & Mobility, Europe and Health. On a comparable
basis, income from operations increased 13.4%.
The effective income tax rate was 32.5%
in 3Q26. Our income tax provision for 3Q26 was Ps. 5,080 million, an increase of 34.2% relative to 3Q25.
Net consolidated income amounted to Ps.
10,569 million, compared to Ps. 5,838 million in 3Q25, representing an increase of 81.0%. This increase was supported by: i) the absence
of the Ps. 3,146 million loss from discontinued operations recorded in 3Q25, which reflected the divestiture of Solistica5;
ii) growth in income from operations; and iii) a non-cash foreign exchange gain of Ps. 1,121 million, compared to a loss of Ps. 1,261
million in 3Q25, reflecting a favorable swing of Ps. 2,382 million, mainly due to the depreciation of the Mexican peso against the U.S.
dollar, that impacted our investment in ImperialBrady as well as our U.S. dollar-denominated cash and investment position. These effects
were partially offset by: i) higher net interest expense of Ps. 4,541 million, compared to Ps. 3,563 million in 3Q25, mainly reflecting
lower interest income of Ps. 1,371 million compared to Ps. 1,907 million in 3Q25, driven by lower interest rates and lower cash balances;
ii) other financial expenses of Ps. 312 million, compared to income of Ps. 55 million in 3Q25, mainly reflecting a loss on financial instruments
at Coca-Cola FEMSA Brazil; and iii) a higher income tax provision of Ps. 5,080 million, compared to Ps. 3,785 million in 3Q25.
Net majority income was Ps. 2.00 per FEMSA
BD Unit6, representing 185.7% growth, and US$1.10 per FEMSA ADS4.
Net Debt / Adjusted EBITDA. On an ex-KOF
basis, as of September 30, 2026, cash and investments were Ps. 70,403 million and total debt was Ps. 165,605 million, resulting in
net debt of Ps. 95,203 million. Our Net Debt / Adjusted LTM EBITDA ratio ex-KOF was 1.19x up from 0.91x in 3Q25. This year-on-year increase
mainly reflects the cash outflow related to our capital allocation strategy.
Capital expenditures amounted to Ps. 9,408
million, representing 4.0% of total revenues and a decrease of 28.3% compared to 3Q25, mainly reflecting lower CAPEX at Coca-Cola FEMSA,
coupled with lower investments at OXXO Mexico, Europe and Health. This was partially offset by higher CAPEX in Americas & Mobility,
related to store expansion investments across the region.

Total revenues increased 10.3% in 3Q26
compared to 3Q25, reflecting a 9.2% increase in same-store sales, coupled with 2.9% store expansion. The growth in same-store sales was
driven by a 5.5% increase in the average ticket and a 3.5% increase in store traffic. During the quarter, results were supported by: i)
the continued execution of commercial initiatives, including assortment and affordability actions across key categories, which contributed
to market share gains in core categories; ii) more favorable weather conditions compared to the prior year; and iii) continued growth
in services. These effects were partially offset by above-trend store closures. The increase in average ticket mainly reflected the effect
of excise taxes on beverages and cigarettes. During the quarter, the OXXO store base in Mexico expanded by 51 net stores. This included
the addition of 191 stores, offset by the closure of 140 older, underperforming locations, as part of a portfolio optimization initiative.
OXXO Mexico added 702 net new stores during the last twelve months and had a total of 24,759 stores as of September 30, 2026.
Gross profit increased 7.4% and reached
44.8% of total revenues, representing a 120-basis point contraction compared to 3Q25. This contraction mainly reflects a demanding comparison
base as the third quarter of 2025 included a particularly high concentration of commercial income. Normalizing for this effect, OXXO Mexico’s
gross margin would have declined in line with the 70-basis point contraction of the second quarter of 2026, reflecting the solid performance
of key impulse categories, and the continued growth in higher margin services. This was partially offset by continued commercial initiatives
in key categories, aimed at strengthening traffic and market share, including adjustments to price package architecture and price rationalization
in relevant traffic-driving categories.
Income from operations increased 5.8% compared
to 3Q25 and reached 9.3% of total revenues, representing a 40-basis point margin contraction year over year. This performance was mainly
explained by the gross margin contraction described above, the costs associated with the closing of underperforming stores, and higher
labor and variable compensation expenses. Operating expenses increased 7.9%, below revenue growth, reflecting the cost containment and
operational efficiencies implemented during last year, particularly in administrative expenses, partially offset by higher labor costs.

AMERICAS1
Total revenues increased 18.1% in 3Q26
compared to 3Q25, reflecting an 8.4% increase in our Fuel sales, coupled with a 66.0% increase in our Merchandise sales. The growth in
our Fuel sales mainly reflects an increase in volume and traffic in our service stations in Mexico, and flat results in our U.S. fuel
business with higher prices being offset by lower volumes. The solid performance in our Merchandise sales mainly reflects the continued
growth of our OXXO operations in South America. We also benefited from the integration of OXXO Brazil which we began consolidating on
February 1st, 2026. Same-store sales increased 13.8% in Mexican pesos and 15.8% on a comparable basis during the quarter, supported
by currency-neutral growth of 5.1% in Brazil, 21.2% in Latam and 1.5% in the U.S. On a comparable basis, which excludes the addition of
OXXO Brazil as well as currency effects, total revenues increased 10.9%. During the quarter, the store base expanded by 33 net stores.
Americas & Mobility had 56 net store additions during the last twelve months and had a total of 1,986 stores as of September 30,
2026.
Gross profit reached 16.8% of total revenues,
representing a 10-basis point margin expansion compared to 3Q25. This includes a reclassification of certain expenses, from cost of goods
sold to selling expenses at OXXO operations in Colombia, Chile and Peru, reflected in 3Q25. Gross profit increased 18.6% compared to 3Q25.
Excluding the effects of this reclassification, gross profit would have increased 25.4% and gross profit margin would have expanded 110
basis points from an adjusted base of 15.7% in 3Q25, mainly reflecting strong growth in Merchandise gross profit driven by the performance
of our OXXO stores in South America, and the consolidation of OXXO Brazil. Fuel gross profit increased 4.4%, reflecting better gross margin
at our Fuel operations in the U.S., partially offset by higher fuel costs coupled with maximum price commitments that continued to weigh
on unit margins in our fuel business in Mexico.
Income from operations decreased by 75.9%
compared to 3Q25 and represented 0.6% of total revenues, representing a 220-basis point margin contraction year over year. This performance
was mainly explained by the incorporation of OXXO Brazil losses into the consolidated results, coupled with the continued pressure on
Fuel margins in Mexico described above. This was partially offset by improved operating performance in OXXO Chile, Peru and Colombia.
Operating expenses increased above total revenue growth, reflecting the incorporation of OXXO Brazil and increased expenses as we continue
to build capabilities to support future growth. On a comparable basis, income from operations decreased 29.9%.

Total revenues increased 8.7% in the first
nine months of 2026 compared to the same period of 2025, driven by growth in Coca-Cola FEMSA, OXXO Mexico, Americas & Mobility
and Health, partially offset by a decrease in Europe. Revenues reflected a net negative foreign exchange effect as the Mexican peso appreciated
relative to other currencies; as a result, revenues grew 9.4% on a comparable basis.
Gross profit increased 8.3%, reflecting
increases at OXXO Mexico, Coca-Cola FEMSA and Americas & Mobility, offset by Europe and Health. Gross margin contracted 20 basis
points, reaching 40.1%. This reflects margin expansion in Coca-Cola FEMSA and Americas & Mobility, offset by contractions in
OXXO Mexico, Europe and Health. It is important to highlight that the contractions in Europe and Health include the reclassification of
distribution expenses from selling expenses to cost of goods sold, which does not impact income from operations. Excluding the effects
of this reclassification, the gross margin for the first nine months of 2025 would have been 39.9%, an expansion of 20 basis points. On
a comparable basis, which accounts for currency effects and M&A, gross profit increased 9.3%.
Income from operations increased 7.9%,
driven by growth in Coca-Cola FEMSA and OXXO Mexico, partially offset by declines in Americas & Mobility, Europe and Health.
The consolidated operating margin stood at 7.9%, contracting 10 basis points year over year, reflecting margin expansion in OXXO Mexico
and Coca-Cola FEMSA, offset by margin contractions in Americas & Mobility, Europe and Health. On a comparable basis, income from
operations increased 12.4%.
The effective income tax rate was 26.7%
for the first nine months of 2026. This is largely explained by a one-time gain related to the BradyPLUS and Imperial Dade merger recorded
in the first quarter of 2026, reflecting a non-cash accounting gain which increased profitability with no current tax effect. Excluding
this impact, the effective income tax rate would be 34.8%. The gap between our effective tax rate and the statutory rate of 30% is mainly
explained by non-deductible expenses, primarily at OXXO Mexico, and non-creditable tax loss effects, mainly from Spin. Our income tax
provision for the first nine months of 2026 was Ps. 13,656 million, an increase of 6.2% relative to the same period of 2025, mainly reflecting
the increase in income from operations.
Net consolidated income amounted to Ps.
37,456 million, representing an increase of 84.0% compared to the first nine months of 2025. This increase primarily reflected a one-time
gain related to ImperialBrady, the result of the merger of BradyPLUS and Imperial Dade, recorded in the first quarter of 2026. Excluding
this one-time gain, our net consolidated income amounted to Ps. 25,504 million, representing an increase of 12.1% compared to the first
nine months of 2025. This increase primarily reflected growth in income from operations, as well as: i) a non-cash foreign exchange gain
of Ps. 21 million, compared to a loss of Ps. 4,917 million in 2025, reflecting a favorable swing of Ps. 4,938 million, mainly due to a
lower appreciation of the Mexican peso against the U.S. dollar and a lower U.S. dollar cash and investment balance; ii) a lower loss from
participation in associates’ results of Ps. 55 million, compared to a loss of Ps. 995 million in 2025, which reflected the results
of our participation in the joint venture in Brazil and our participation in ImperialBrady; and iii) the absence of the Ps. 813 million
loss from discontinued operations2 recorded in the first nine months of 2025. These effects were partially offset by: i) higher
net interest expense of Ps. 12,865 million, compared to Ps. 9,822 million, mainly reflecting lower interest income of Ps. 4,053 million
compared to Ps. 6,086 million, driven by lower interest rates and a lower cash and investment balance; ii) other financial expenses of
Ps. 226 million, compared to income of Ps. 1,856 million in 2025, which reflected the valuation effect recorded in 2025 related to HKN
shares; and iii) a higher income tax provision of Ps. 13,656 million, compared to Ps. 12,855 million.
Net majority income per FEMSA BD Unit3
was Ps. 7.97 (US$4.41 per ADS4).
Capital expenditures amounted to Ps. 24,476
million, representing 3.6% of total revenues and a decrease of 21.3% compared to the first nine months of 2025, mainly reflecting lower
CAPEX at Coca-Cola FEMSA, driven by a more selective approach to capital deployment, and at OXXO Mexico, reflecting a more measured pace
of store openings compared to the prior year, coupled with lower investments in Health and Europe. This was partially offset by higher
CAPEX in Americas & Mobility, mainly reflecting the addition of OXXO Brazil to the comparison base and continued expansion investments
across the region.

ABOUT FEMSA
FEMSA is a company that creates economic and social
value through companies and institutions and strives to be the best employer and neighbor to the communities in which it operates. It
participates in two core sectors, retail and beverages. In retail, FEMSA is present through four divisions: i) OXXO Mexico, operating
the largest small-format store chain in Mexico; ii) Americas & Mobility, which includes its OXXO convenience store operations
across Latin America and the United States, as well as its gas station business in Mexico and the United States; iii) Europe, operating
convenience and foodvenience formats in five European countries; and iv) FEMSA Health, which includes drugstores and related activities
in four Latin American countries. In Mexico, OXXO’s operations are enhanced by, and comprise a customer-focused ecosystem with Spin,
a digital platform that leverages the OXXO store network to provide Mexican consumers with access to digital financial services, including
Spin by OXXO and Spin Premia, among other initiatives. In the beverage sector, FEMSA participates through Coca-Cola FEMSA, the largest
franchise bottler of Coca-Cola products in the world by volume. Across its business units, FEMSA has more than 369,000 employees in 18
countries. FEMSA is a member of the Dow Jones Best-in-Class World Index & Dow Jones Best-in-Class MILA Pacific Alliance
Index, both from S&P Global; FTSE4Good Emerging Index; MSCI EM Latin America ESG Leaders Index; S&P/BMV Total México ESG,
among other indexes.

DEFINITIONS
Volume
is expressed in unit cases. Unit case refers to 192 ounces of finished beverage product (24 eight-ounce
servings) and, when applied to soda fountains, refers to the volume of syrup, powders, and concentrate that is required to produce 192
ounces of finished beverage product.
Transactions
refer to the number of single units (e.g., a can or a bottle) sold, regardless of their size or volume
or whether they are sold individually or in multipacks, except for soda fountains, which represent multiple transactions based on a standard
12 oz. serving.
Operating
income is a non-GAAP financial measure computed as “gross profit – operating expenses
– other operating expenses, net + operative equity method (gain) loss in associates.”
Adjusted
EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure computed as “operating income + depreciation + amortization &
other operating non-cash charges.”
Earnings
per share are equal to “quarterly earnings / shares outstanding.” Earnings per share
(EPS) are calculated using 16,806,658,096 shares outstanding. For the convenience of the reader, as a KOFUBL Unit is comprised of 8 shares
(3 Series B shares and 5 Series L shares), earnings per unit are equal to EPS multiplied by 8. Each ADS represents 10 KOFUBL
Units.
COMPARABILITY
Our “comparable”
term means, with respect to a year-over-year comparison, the change of a given measure excluding translation effects resulting from exchange
rate movements. In preparing this measure, management has used its best judgment, estimates, and assumptions to maintain comparability.
Due to the average
depreciation of the Argentine peso and most of the operating currencies relative to the Mexican peso in the third quarter of 2026, as
compared to the same period of 2025, we had an unfavorable currency translation effect into Mexican pesos. Please see page 17 for
exchange rate fluctuations.
