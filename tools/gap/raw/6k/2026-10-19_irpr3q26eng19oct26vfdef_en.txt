GRUPO AEROPORTUARIO DEL PACIFICO
ANNOUNCES RESULTS FOR THE THIRD QUARTER OF | 2026
Guadalajara, Jalisco, Mexico, October 19, 2026 – Grupo Aeroportuario del Pacífico, S.A.B. de C.V.
(NYSE: PAC; BMV: GAP) (“the Company” or “GAP”) reports its consolidated results for the third
quarter ended September 30, 2026 (3Q26). The results presented in this report include the effects of
the business combination effective May 1, 2026. The figures are unaudited and have been
prepared following International Financial Reporting Standards (“IFRS”) as issued by the
International Accounting Standards Board (“IASB”).
Summary of Results 3Q26 vs. 3Q25
The sum of aeronautical and non-aeronautical services revenues increased by
Ps. 1,041.6 million, or 13.2%. Total revenues increased by Ps. 2,091.2 million, or 21.8%.
Excluding the impact of the incorporation of Cross Border Xpress (“CBX”) operations,
aeronautical and non-aeronautical services revenues would have increased by Ps | 266.1
million, or 3.4%, while total revenues would have increased by Ps. 1,315.7 million, or 13.7%.
Cost of services increased by Ps. 409.2 million, or 26.8%. Excluding the impact of the
incorporation of CBX operations, the cost of services would have increased by Ps | 192.5
million, or 12.6%.
Income from operations increased by Ps. 897.7 million, or 21.6%. Excluding the
contribution from CBX operations, operating income would have increased by Ps | 382.4
million, or 9.2%.
EBITDA increased by Ps. 979.0 million, or 19.2%, an increase from Ps. 5,085.6 million in
3Q25 to Ps. 6,064.6 million in 3Q26. EBITDA margin (excluding the effects of IFRIC-12) went
from 64.3% in 3Q25 to 67.8% in 3Q26.
Comprehensive income increased by Ps. 807.3 million, or 32.8%, from an income of
Ps. 2,457.8 million in 3Q25 to an income of Ps. 3,265.1 million in 3Q26.

Business Combination:
As of May 1, 2026, GAP’s consolidated results reflect the effects of the business combination,
which included the integration of CBX operations and the internalization of technical
assistance and technology transfer services. This transaction was approved by the
Shareholders’ Meeting held on December 11, 2025, and formalized through the execution of
the merger agreement signed on April 30, 2026.
The Company is currently in the process of determining the fair values of the assets acquired
and liabilities assumed as a result of the business combination. Accordingly, the amounts
related to this transaction presented in the consolidated financial statements included in this
report are preliminary and subject to adjustment.

Revenues (3Q26 vs. 3Q25)
Aeronautical services revenues increased by Ps. 142.0 million, or 2.6%.
Non-aeronautical services revenues increased by Ps. 899.6 million, or 37.0%.
Revenues from improvements to concession assets increased by Ps. 1,049.6 million, or
62.8%.
Total revenues increased by Ps. 2,091.2 million, or 21.8%.
The change in aeronautical services revenues was primarily due to the following factors:
Revenues from the Mexican airports increased by Ps. 247.7 million, or 5.3%, compared to
3Q25, primarily driven by the gradual implementation of the maximum tariffs approved for
the 2025–2029 regulatory period and an increase in passenger traffic of 0.4%. This growth
was partially offset by the 7.5% appreciation of the Mexican peso against the U.S. dollar,
which negatively impacted revenues from international tariffs denominated in U.S. dollars.
Revenues from the Jamaican airports decreased by Ps. 105.7 million, or 12.7%, compared
to 3Q25, mainly due to a 15.8% decrease in passenger traffic during the quarter. In addition,
the appreciation of the Mexican peso against the U.S. dollar negatively affected the
translation of revenues.
The change in non-aeronautical services revenues was primarily driven by the following factors:
Revenues from the Mexican airports increased by Ps. 163.2 million, or 7.6%, compared to
3Q25. Revenues from businesses operated directly by us increased by Ps. 164.0 million, or
14.2%, while revenues from businesses operated by third parties decreased by Ps | 0.8
million, or 1.4%.
Revenues from the Jamaican airports decreased by Ps. 39.1 million, or 13.2%, compared to
3Q25, primarily due to the decline in passenger traffic and the peso appreciation against
the U.S. dollar during 3Q26.
Revenues generated by CBX amounted to Ps. 775.5 million, equivalent to US$44.6 million.
During this period, a total of 1,034,274 passengers used the facilities in both directions,
representing an average revenue of US$43.1 per user.

Total operating costs increased by Ps. 1,193.5 million, or 22.0%, compared to 3Q25, mainly due
to Ps. 1,049.6 million, or 62.8%, increase in the cost of improvements to concession assets (IFRIC
12); a Ps. 409.2 million, or 26.8%, increase in cost of services; and a Ps. 81.2 million, or 8.7%, increase
in depreciation and amortization. These increases were partially offset by Ps. 183.3 million, or 81.0%,
decrease in technical assistance fees, and Ps. 148.6 million decrease in concession fees, or 13.8%.
Excluding the cost of improvements to concession assets (IFRIC 12), operating expenses
increased by Ps. 143.9 million, or 3.8%, compared to 3Q25.
The changes in total operating costs were primarily due to the following factors:
Mexican airports:
Operating costs increased by Ps. 993.0 million, or 22.7%, compared to 3Q25, primarily
due to a Ps. 970.3 million increase in the cost of improvements to concession assets (IFRIC
12); a Ps. 223.8 million increase in cost of services; and a Ps. 42.1 million increase in
depreciation and amortization. These increases were partially offset by Ps. 183.3 million
decrease in technical assistance fees, and Ps. 46.0 million in concession fees.
The change in the cost of services at our Mexican airports during 3Q26 was mainly due to:
Employee costs increased by Ps. 84.7 million, or 14.8%, mainly due to an increase in
operational personnel at the airports and bonded warehouse, as well as salary adjustments.
Maintenance increased by Ps. 35.2 million, or 13.5%, mainly due to the opening of new
operational areas, and airfield maintenance.
Safety, security, and insurance increased by Ps. 24.0 million, or 13.1%, mainly due to an
increase in security personnel headcount, minimum wage adjustments, and higher
insurance costs related to goods safeguarded within the bonded warehouse.
Jamaican Airports:
Operating expenses decreased by Ps. 58.5 million, or 4.2%, compared to 3Q25, mainly
due to a decrease of Ps. 102.6 million, or 22.7%, in concession fees, resulting from lower
revenues at Montego Bay airport; a Ps. 31.3 million, or 13.6%, decrease in cost of services;
and a Ps. 3.8 million, or 2.7%, decrease in depreciation and amortization. These decreases
were partially offset by Ps. 79.4 million, or 36.3%, increase in the cost of improvements to
concession assets (IFRIC 12).

Cross Border Xpress:
CBX’s operating expenses amounted to Ps. 260.2 million during 3Q26, primarily
consisting of cost of services of Ps. 216.7 million and depreciation and amortization of
Ps. 42.9 million.
Operating income margin remained at 43.3% in both quarters. Excluding the effects of IFRIC-12, the
operating income margin increased from 52.5% in 3Q25 to 56.4% in 3Q26. Income from operations
increased by Ps. 897.7 million, or 21.6%, compared to 3Q25, with CBX contributing Ps. 515.3 million.
EBITDA margin decreased from 53.1% in 3Q25 to 52.0% in 3Q26. Excluding the effects of IFRIC-12,
EBITDA margin increased from 64.3% in 3Q25 to 67.8% in 3Q26. EBITDA increased by Ps | 979.0
million, or 19.2%, compared to 3Q25. EBITDA margin growth, excluding the effects of IFRIC 12, was
partially offset by the impact on the Jamaican airports from the appreciation of the Mexican peso and
lower passenger traffic. CBX contributed Ps. 558.2 million, with an EBITDA margin of 72.0%.
Financial results increased expenses by Ps. 381.3 million, or 57.6%, going from a net expense of
Ps. 661.8 million in 3Q25 to a net expense of Ps. 1,043.1 million in 3Q26. This change was mainly the
result of:
- Foreign exchange gains increased from Ps. 60.9 million in 3Q25 to Ps. 114.1 million in 3Q26,
resulting in an additional foreign exchange gain of Ps. 53.2 million due to the appreciation of
the Mexican peso. Additionally, the foreign currency translation effect resulted in a gain of Ps.
584.8 million.
- Interest expense increased by Ps. 473.9 million, or 41.8%, compared to 3Q25,
primarily due to higher debt levels to finance airport CAPEX and the acquisition of a | 25%
equity interest in CBX.
- Interest income increased by Ps. 38.9 million, or 18.2%, compared to 3Q25, mainly due to
the increase in cash and cash equivalents.
In 3Q26, net and comprehensive income increased by Ps. 807.3 million, or 32.8%, compared to
3Q25, primarily driven by a Ps. 516.5 million, or 14.8%, increase in income before taxes, mainly
attributable to the increase in EBITDA, as previously described.
Net income increased by Ps. 218.1 million, or 8.1%, compared to 3Q25. Income tax increased by
Ps. 298.3 million, or 37.7%, reflecting a Ps. 129.1 million increase in current income tax and a Ps.
169.2 million decrease in the deferred tax benefit. This was mainly attributable to a lower inflationary
effect, as inflation decreased from 0.7% in 3Q25 to 0.4% in 3Q26, and a Ps. 88.4 million decrease in
the recognition of tax loss carryforwards compared to the same quarter of 2025.

Company Description
Grupo Aeroportuario del Pacífico, S.A.B. de C.V. (GAP) operates 12 airports throughout Mexico’s Pacific region, including the major cities of
Guadalajara and Tijuana, the four tourist destinations of Puerto Vallarta, Los Cabos, La Paz and Manzanillo, and six other mid-sized cities:
Hermosillo, Guanajuato, Morelia, Aguascalientes, Mexicali, and Los Mochis. In February 2006, GAP’s shares were listed on the New York
Stock Exchange under the ticker symbol “PAC” and on the Mexican Stock Exchange under the ticker symbol “GAP”. In April 2015, GAP
acquired 100% of Desarrollo de Concesiones Aeroportuarias, S.L., which owns a majority stake in MBJ Airports Limited, a company
operating Sangster International Airport in Montego Bay, Jamaica. In October 2018, GAP entered into a concession agreement for the
Norman Manley International Airport operation in Kingston, Jamaica, and took control of the operation in October 2019. In May 2026, GAP
completed a business combination pursuant to which it acquired full ownership of the Cross Border Xpress (“CBX”), a cross-border terminal
located in San Diego, California and connected to the Tijuana International Airport.
