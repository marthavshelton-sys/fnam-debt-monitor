The third quarter marked an important milestone for ASUR. On September 1st, we
completed the acquisition of CPC Aeroportos from Motiva which added 20 airports to our
portfolio, the majority of which are located in Brazil. Coupled with our December | 2025
acquisition of U.S. commercial operations we have taken significant actions in diversifying

ASUR’s operations. In turn, ASUR has evolved into the leading international airport operator
across the Americas.
Today, our portfolio spans 36 airports across seven countries, complemented by commercial
operations at three major U.S. airports. To put this scale in perspective, the combined airport
portfolio served approximately 120 million passengers in 2025 on a pro forma basis up from
70 million we reported last year. We now have much broader business than the Mexican
airport group that was established nearly three decades ago.
CPC Aeroportos adds 17 airports in Brazil and one each in Ecuador, Costa Rica and Curazao.
This gives us an established presence in Brazil, Latin America’s largest aviation market, and
expands our operations into three additional markets. The final purchase price was 5.5 billion
reais, or approximately 992 million U.S. dollars, after customary closing adjustments. Our
results for the third quarter include operations solely for the month of September which we
consolidated
are under one region called “Brazil & Others”.
A larger, more diverse company our operations are now spread across more markets with
different passenger profiles reducing our dependence on one market. It also provides more
opportunities to apply the operating and commercial experience we have built over many
years. While we do not expect to capture significant operating synergies, our focus now is on
implementing seamless integration while maintaining our disciplined approach to investment.
We are making changes to our organizational structure to better support the large growing
business. To that end, on August 20th, shareholders approved bringing in-house the technical
assistance and technology transfer services provided by ITA. Once completed, the merger will
bring these capabilities and personnel directly into ASUR, simplifying our structure and
keeping the expertise within the Company. As a reminder, we will assume the costs of
providing these services and eliminate the recurring external technical assistance fee, which
amounted to approximately 401 million pesos in 2025.
Our U.S. commercial business gives us another avenue for growth. At JFK, LAX and
Chicago O’Hare, we are building a platform in non-regulated, dollar-denominated commercial
revenues. The next major project is the New Terminal One, expected to be open toward the
end of the first quarter of next year. We also continue developing commercial expenses at
LAX to be ready for the Olympic Games in 2028. The business today is only a small
contribution of what we expect the longer term potential of this new business to be.
While we are very excited about these new opportunities, at the same time, Mexico
remains central to our business and our investment program. Within Mexico, Cancún, is an
important market for us with significant investments underway to improve operational
efficiencies and support future growth. We are taking a long-term view of Cancún’s
development while managing the current operating environment.
First, the expected remodeled and expanded Terminal 1 construction will be completed during
this fourth quarter and then we will proceed with the Operational Readiness and Airport

Transfer to start operations in the first quarter. Terminal 1 will allow us to redistribute
passenger flows and relieve pressure on Terminal 2. It will also create additional commercial
capacity capabilities and improve the passenger experience.
We are also investing in Terminal 4 with the project expected to be operational at year end
2028. This investment will provide 12 additional boarding gates, on top of the four added
during 2027 and three more due to the expansion of T1, a connecting taxiway and related
infrastructure.
Our capital allocation continues to balance investment in the business to support growth and
improve efficiencies with returns to shareholders, there are two extraordinary dividends of | 15
pesos each, payable on November 30th and December 23rd.
Turning to our energy strategy, during the third quarter we began procuring clean energy
from a third-party supplier in Mexico. We purchased 13.1 million kilowatt-hours, representing
more than 40% of electricity consumption at our Mexican airports during the quarter. We also
began the executive design of additional solar panels in our parking facilities. There are
expected to become operational in 2027 and could generate an additional 8 million kilowatt-
hours of renewable energy annually.
Our efforts to advance renewable energy also earned recognition during the quarter, with
Airports Council International awarding the U.S. Green Airport Recognition for our “Digital
Renewable Energy Management System” project.
Moving on to passenger traffic, total traffic decreased 1.2% to 20.9 million passengers
and includes one month contribution from Brazil & Others, considering September 2025 data
for comparison purposes. Lower traffic in Mexico and Puerto Rico was partially offset by growth
in Colombia and the acquired portfolio.
● Mexico, traffic declined nearly 5%, primarily due to weaker international traffic at Cancún.
Growth in domestic traffic across our other eight Mexican airports partially offset this
weakness. Airline capacity constraints including Sprit Airlines bankruptcy and higher jet
fuel prices continue to weigh on demand, particularly for international travel. Passenger
volumes by region to and from: the United States, Europe, South America decreased by
14.9%, 14.4%, 7.9% respectively, while Canada increased 5.7%.
● In Puerto Rico, traffic declined slightly over 3% due to continued impact from the
bankruptcy of Spirit airlines, early May and softness in both domestic and international
demand. On a positive note, September showed an improvement, with total traffic
increasing just over 3% following declines in July and August.
● Colombia continued to outperform the rest of the portfolio with traffic up just over | 5%
reflecting strength in both domestic and international traffic benefiting from better
connectivity and healthy demand.

● Total traffic figures for the quarter also included one month contribution from the

4 million passengers in September. This mainly driven by low single digits growth Brazil
and Ecuador, more than offsetting mid-to-low single digit declines in Costa Rica and
Curazao.
On a bigger picture macro front, we don’t see any near term improvement as conditions
remain challenging, particularly for international traffic in Mexico although airline schedules
for the winter season indicate only more seat capacity for December than a year ago, which
is not encouraging for the whole winter season. The September improvement in Puerto Rico
is encouraging, while Colombia continues to grow. We remain cautious on the pace of recovery
while at the same are focused on supporting the long-term demand fundamentals across our
broader portfolio and with CPC we diminish the dependance in Mexico and basically in one
Airport.
Turning next to the financial performance. As a reminder, the figures I will discuss
exclude construction revenue and construction costs, unless otherwise noted.
Revenues increased nearly 13% to 7.8 billion pesos. Non-aeronautical revenues grew just
under 27%, including 456 million pesos from our U.S. operations, 362 million pesos from the
first month of consolidation of Brazil & Others and 16% growth in Colombia, offsetting the
softer performance in Mexico and Puerto Rico.
Aeronautical revenues were up 5% to 4.8 billion pesos, including a 315 million peso
contribution from Brazil & Others. Higher passenger traffic supported higher aeronautical
revenues in Colombia, while Puerto Rico’s aeronautical revenues increased primarily due to
the exchange rate difference. These gains were partly offset by softer traffic in Mexico.
Commercial revenue per passenger increased 3.5% to 130.5 pesos, mainly reflecting the
contribution of our U.S. commercial operations. Puerto Rico posted the highest commercial
revenues per passenger at 163.3 pesos, up 2%, followed by Mexico with 129 pesos although
this was 10% lower year on year mainly reflecting lower traffic and passenger mix. Colombia
delivered double-digit growth reaching 65.2 pesos while Brazil & Others added 64.7 pesos in
commercial revenues per passenger.
We continue expanding our commercial footprint. Over the past twelve months, we opened
39 new commercial spaces across Mexico, Puerto Rico and Colombia: 31 in Colombia, seven
in Puerto Rico and one in Mexico.
Moving on to profitability, consolidated EBITDA increased 1.7% to 4.7 billion pesos. Brazil
& Others contributed 313 million pesos in September and our U.S. commercial operations
added 49 million pesos in the quarter. This, together with increases of 7% in Puerto Rico
driven by lower operating costs and nearly 2% in Colombia, more than offset a 10% decline
in Mexico.

Adjusted EBITDA margin declined 7.4 percentage points year-on-year to 60.3%. This
reflect the pressure of Mexico's revenues together with the consolidation of Brazil and Others
and the U.S., both of lower margins. Brazil & Other reported an Adjusted EBITDA margin of
46.3% for September 2026, while the U.S. reported an EBITDA margin of close to 11%. Recall
this business has a different cost structure from our airport operations, including lease
obligations to airport authorities, and the margin is not directly comparable. We are also
incurring expenses for projects that are not yet generating revenues, including commercial
spaces at our U.S. operations which are expected to open until 2027 and 2028.
On the balance sheet, we closed the quarter with cash and cash equivalents of 20 billion
pesos and total debt of nearly 70 billion pesos. Of this, 26.5% was denominated in Mexican
pesos, 46.4% in U.S. dollars corresponding mainly to Puerto Rico, and 27.1% in Brazilian
reais.
Net debt to last-twelve-month EBITDA was 2.6 times, up from 0.2 times, reflecting the
acquisition financing and the addition of debt from the commercial operation in U.S. and Brazil
& Others. After quarter-end, Cancún Airport placed US$1.8 billion in senior notes in two
tranches: US$900 million due in 2031 and US$900 million in 2036. These notes carried an
investment grade rating by S&P and Fitch. We used the net proceeds to repay the bridge
facilities for the U.S. and CPC acquisitions, repaying the remaining Santander loan and
partially prepay the BBVA loan. This refinancing extends our debt maturities by 7 years on
average.
Lastly, capital expenditures totaled 2.7 billion pesos during the quarter, with the majority
expended in Mexico as we advance our Master Development Program.
At yesterday’s exchange rate converted to Mexican pesos for each region, next year we expect
to invest close to 6 billion pesos in Mexico, 1.8 billion in Costa Rica, 1.7 billion in Puerto Rico,
1.5 in Brazil, 414 million in our U.S. commercial operation, 230 million in Colombia and | 213
in Curacao. We expect to fund all of these with resources generated by the operation of the
region, except for Puerto Rico. This is a total of 11.8 billion pesos
Main projects include the start of the second phase of the expansion of Terminal 4 at Cancún
Airport, the beginning of the construction of a second runway at Curitiba Airport to be
completed in 2029, runway refurbishment at Belo Horizonte, Goiania, Teresina and San Luis
airports in Brazil which expected to complete in 2027. Also to be completed in 2027 is the
first phase of the terminal expansion at San José Airport in Costa Rica, also a hangar
relocation
that will allow for the second phase expansion of this terminal in 2028..
Wrapping up, ASUR is making consistent progress in executing its strategic priorities,
optimizing its structure and further diversifying its geographic operations. We are
accomplishing this in a challenging operating environment particularly in Mexico, and we
remain cautious on the pace of recovery. At the same time, we completed an important
acquisition, obtained approval for a more efficient corporate structure and refinanced our
acquisition bridge loans with long-term debt. Our broader airport portfolio, U.S. commercial
platform and continued investment in capacity provide ASUR additional opportunities for long-
