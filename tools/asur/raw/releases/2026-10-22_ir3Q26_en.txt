Recent Events
Internalization of the Technical Assistance and Technology Transfer Services and Extraordinary
Dividends
On August 20, 2026, ASUR held a Shareholders’ Meeting which approved the integration of the technical assistance
and technology transfer services, via the merger of Inversiones y Técnicas Aeroportuarias, S.A.P.I. de C.V., ASUR’s
current strategic partner. Consequently, ASUR expects to issue and put into circulation approximately 7.2 million
new net shares in its capital stock. By virtue of the merger, ASUR’s shares in circulation would increase to
approximately 307.3 million from 300.0 million.
Shareholders also approved the payment of two extraordinary cash dividends from the share buyback reserve of
Ps.10.00 per share each, payable on November 30, 2026, and December 23, 2026, respectively, as well as
amendments to the Company’s bylaws to reflect the current regulatory framework and the proposed
internalization.
ASUR Closes Acquisition of CPC Aeroportos
On September 1, 2026, after all conditions precedent to the agreement entered into on November 18, 2025, had
been satisfied, ASUR and Motiva announced the completion of the acquisition of Motiva’s entire equity interest in
Companhia de Participações em Concessões (“CPC Aeroportos”), a Motiva subsidiary, for a purchase price of R$5.1
billion (US$992.2 million), following customary closing adjustments. The transaction adds 20 airports across four
markets in Latin America and the Caribbean to ASUR’s portfolio, including Brazil, Latin America’s largest aviation
market by passenger traffic.
Subsequent Events
Successful Placement by Cancun Airport of Senior Notes Rated Investment Grade
On October 8, 2026, ASUR announced the successful placement, through its subsidiary Aeropuerto de Cancún,
S.A. de C.V., of US$900 million Senior Notes bearing interest at 6.711% and maturing in 2031, and US$900
million Senior Notes bearing interest at 7.491% and maturing in 2036. The notes were rated BBB by S&P Global
Ratings and BBB+ by Fitch Ratings. ASUR used the net proceeds to repay or refinance in full the Ps.6,390 million
and US$1,230 million outstanding under the bridge facilities related to the acquisitions of ASUR US Airports and
CPC Aeroportos, respectively, in addition to the Ps.675 million loan entered into Banco Santander, a partial
prepayment of Ps.3,000 million on the Ps.9500.0 bank loan from BBVA Mexico, together with legal fees, interest
and fees in connection with these financings.

Passenger Traffic
ASUR’s total passenger traffic decreased 1.2% YoY to 20.9 million passengers in 3Q26.
Passenger traffic in Mexico decreased 4.6% YoY to 9.1 million passengers, reflecting a decrease of 11.8% in
international traffic, partially offset by an increase of 1.6% in domestic traffic. In Puerto Rico, passenger traffic
decreased 3.2% YoY to 3.2 million passengers, reflecting decreases of 3.1% and 3.9% in domestic and
international traffic, respectively. In Colombia, passenger traffic increased 5.2% YoY to 4.7 million passengers,
reflecting increases of 6.5% and 1.0% in domestic and international traffic, respectively.
During September 2026, total passenger traffic in Brazil increased 2.6% YoY, driven by a 2.9% increase in
domestic traffic, partially offset by an 11.5% decrease in international traffic. Traffic in Ecuador increased 1.7%,
with increases of 0.1% and 3.2% in domestic and international traffic, while traffic in Costa Rica decreased | 6.1%
YoY reflecting a 6.4% decrease in international traffic, partially offset by a 16.3% increase in domestic traffic. In
Curazao, traffic decreased 0.9%, driven by a 1.6% decrease in international traffic, partially offset by an | 8.9%
increase in domestic passengers.

Review of Consolidated Results
ASUR's consolidated 3Q26 and 9M26 results include the operations of ASUR US Airports, the Company’s U.S.
commercial operation, which was acquired in December 2025 and was not part of the consolidated results in 3Q25.
Accordingly, YoY comparisons reflect the expanded scope of consolidation, primarily affecting non-aeronautical
revenues, operating costs, and margins.
On September 1, 2026, ASUR closed the acquisition of CPC Aeroportos, adding to its portfolio 17 airports in Brazil,
and one each in Costa Rica, Curazao, and Ecuador (“Brazil & Others”), and starting on that date ASUR began to
fully consolidate its operations on a line by line basis. From the acquisition date through September | 30, | 2026,
these operations contributed revenues of Ps.841.2 million and EBITDA of Ps.313.2 million.

Consolidated Operating Costs and Expenses
Consolidated Operating Costs and Expenses, including Construction Costs, increased 28.8% YoY, or
Ps.1,460.3 million, to Ps.6,530.8 million in 3Q26. Excluding Construction Costs, operating costs and expenses
increased 20.9%, or Ps.681.6 million. By region:
•
Mexico: increased 2.2%, or Ps.37.3 million, primarily reflecting higher salaries and wages, professional
fees, security and cleaning services, depreciation and amortization, provisions for doubtful accounts, and
maintenance expenses. These increases were partly offset by lower concession fees, electricity costs,
technical assistance fees, cost of sales related to ASUR’s directly operated commercial operations, insurance
and surety bonds, and tax recoveries and adjustments.
•
Puerto Rico: decreased 5.6%, or Ps.43.5 million, primarily due to lower concession fees, electricity costs,
taxes and fees, maintenance and conservation, insurance and surety bonds, as well as foreign currency
translation effects.
•
Colombia: decreased 7.7%, or Ps.62.5 million, mainly due to the favorable impact of the change in the
concession amortization method in 3Q25. This was partially offset by higher personnel costs, security and
cleaning services, electricity costs, and maintenance and conservation.
• United States: operating costs and expenses totaled Ps.294.1 million, primarily comprised of lease
expenses payable to airport authorities, personnel costs, professional fees, and insurance and surety bond
costs. These were partially offset by favorable accounting effects related to lease agreements with U.S.
airport authorities under IFRS 16.
•
Brazil & Others: Operating costs totaled Ps.456.1 million, primarily comprising depreciation and
amortization, concession fees, salaries and wages, electricity costs, security services, maintenance and
conservation, cleaning services, professional fees, insurance and surety bond costs, taxes and fees, and
materials and supplies.
Cost of Services increased 39.6%, or Ps.596.9 million, during the quarter, primarily due to higher personnel
costs, security and cleaning services, professional fees, insurance and surety bond costs, lease payments to airport
authorities, and provisions for doubtful accounts. These increases were partially offset by lower maintenance and
electricity costs, as well as the favorable accounting impact of lease agreements with U.S. airport authorities under
IFRS 16.
Construction Costs increased 43.0%, or Ps.778.7 million, reflecting increases of 32.4%, or Ps.529.1 million, in
Mexico, a Ps.164.9 million contribution from Brazil & Others, together with an increase Ps.134.7 million in
Colombia. This was partially offset by a 28.1%, or Ps.50.0 million, decrease in Puerto Rico.
Administrative Expenses increased 102.8%, or Ps.89.7 million, primarily reflecting a Ps.66.1 million
contribution from Brazil & Others.
Technical Assistance Fees decreased 31.6%.
Consolidated Concession Fees increased 3.3%, primarily reflecting a Ps.35.7 million contribution from Brazil &
Others.
Depreciation and Amortization increased 0.2%, or Ps.2.0 million. This was primarily due to a 12.2%, or Ps.37.8
million, increase in Mexico; a Ps.93.0 million contribution from Brazil & Others; and a 0.8%, or Ps.1.5 million,
increase in Puerto Rico. These increases were partially offset by a 29.9%, or Ps.130.4 million, decrease in
Colombia, due to the adjustment to the concession amortization method.

Definitions
Concession Services Agreements (IFRIC 12 interpretation). In Mexico, Puerto Rico, and Brazil & Others,
ASUR is required by IFRIC 12 to include in its income statement an income line, “Construction Revenues,”
reflecting the revenue from construction of, or improvements to concessioned assets made during the relevant
period. The same amount is recognized under the expense line “Construction Costs” because ASUR hires third
parties to provide construction services. Because equal amounts of Construction Revenues and Construction Costs
have been included in ASUR's income statement as a result of the application of IFRIC 12, the amount of
Construction Revenues does not have an impact on EBITDA, but it does have an impact on EBITDA Margin. In
Colombia, “Construction Revenues” include the recognition of the revenue to which the concessionaire is entitled
for carrying out the infrastructure works in the development of the concession, while “Construction Costs”
represents the actual costs incurred in the execution of such additions or improvements to the concessioned
assets.
Majority Net Income reflects ASUR’s equity interests in each of its subsidiaries and therefore excludes the | 40%
interest in Aerostar that is owned by other shareholders, as well as 53.5% in Ecuador, 20.2% in Curazao, | 2.5%
in Costa Rica and 61.7% in Bello Horizonte. Other than these operations, ASUR owns (directly or indirectly) | 100%
of its subsidiaries.
EBITDA is calculated as consolidated net income plus income tax, share of loss of investments accounted for
using the equity method, extraordinary items, comprehensive financing costs and depreciation and amortization.
EBITDA should not be considered an alternative to net income as an indicator of our operating performance or an
alternative to cash flow as an indicator of liquidity. Our management believes that EBITDA provides a useful
measure that is widely used by investors and analysts to evaluate our performance and compare it with other
companies. EBITDA is not defined under U.S. GAAP or IFRS and may be calculated differently by different
companies.
Adjusted EBITDA is calculated as consolidated EBITDA adjusted by certain items that include the impact of
IFRS16 in connection with ASUR Airports and add back extraordinary items.
Adjusted EBITDA Margin is calculated by dividing Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA by Total Revenue Excluding
Construction Services Revenue for Mexico, Colombia, Puerto Rico and Brazil & Others, and exclude the effect of
IFRIC 12 with respect to the construction of, or improvements to, concessioned assets. IFRIC 12 requires ASUR
to include in its income statement a revenue line reflecting revenues from the construction or improvement of
concession assets during the relevant period. The same amount is recognized under the expense line “Construction
Costs,” as ASUR engages third parties to provide construction services. In Mexico, Puerto Rico, Colombia and
Brazil & Others, construction revenues and construction costs are included in ASUR’s income statement as a result
of the application of IFRIC 12. Construction revenues therefore have no impact on EBITDA but affect the EBITDA
margin, as the increase in revenues attributable to construction revenues does not result in a corresponding
increase in EBITDA. Like EBITDA Margin, Adjusted EBITDA Margin should not be considered an indicator of our
operating performance or an alternative to cash flow as an indicator of liquidity. It is not defined under U.S. GAAP
or IFRS and may be calculated differently by different companies.
About ASUR
Grupo Aeroportuario del Sureste, S.A.B. de C.V. (ASUR) is a leading international airport operator with a diversified
portfolio of 36 airport concessions across the Americas and a commercial services platform in the U.S.
ASUR operates nine airports in southeastern Mexico, including Cancún Airport, the largest tourist gateway in
Mexico, the Caribbean, and Latin America; and six airports in northern Colombia, including Medellin international
airport (Rionegro), the second busiest in Colombia. The Company also holds a controlling interest in Aerostar
Airport Holdings, LLC, operator of Luis Muñoz Marin International Airport in San Juan, the capital of Puerto Rico,
the island’s primary international gateway. San Juan Airport was the first and remains the only major airport in
the U.S. to have successfully completed a public–private partnership under the FAA Pilot Program. ASUR has
recently acquired 100% of CPC Aeroportos’ stake to operate 17 airports in Brazil, Latin America’s largest aviation
market by passenger traffic, as well as one airport each in Costa Rica, Curazao, and Ecuador.
ASUR also operates a commercial services platform in the U.S. through ASUR Airports, which partners with airports
and airlines to deliver enhanced retail and passenger experiences. ASUR Airports operates at major U.S. hubs,
including Los Angeles International, Chicago O’Hare, and John F. Kennedy International.
